# Frontier Review: Structure-Conditioned Function, Generative Forecasting, and Edge-Level Concept Attribution in Brain Graphs

*Prepared for NeuroLens-RAG. Scope: recent (2022-2026) literature on (A) structural-connectome-as-substrate representation learning, (B) generative forecasting of neural time series, and (C) edge-level concept/graph explainability on brain connectomes, with a synthesis of where the project's specific combination (DTI structural graph + HCP 7T movie-watching functional dynamics + concept-based interpretability + generative forecasting) sits relative to the frontier.*

## Executive Summary

No single published system combines all four ingredients the project has in hand — a subject-specific DTI structural connectome, naturalistic movie-watching functional dynamics, concept-based (CAV/TCAV-style) interpretability, and generative forecasting — and that combination looks like a genuinely open, currently unoccupied position rather than a saturated one. Each pairwise or triple sub-combination, however, already exists in some form: structure-conditioned functional representation learning is an active, crowded area (graph-informed GNNs, structure-function coupling/decoupling metrics, contrastive structure-function alignment); generative forecasting of fMRI dynamics has moved fast in the last 12-18 months, up to and including a structural-prior-conditioned diffusion transformer (BrainWorld, 2026) that is evaluated on HCP movie-watching data but conditions on structural *MRI* (anatomical morphometry), not a DTI-tractography connectivity *graph*; and edge-level explainability on brain graphs is a mature sub-literature (BrainNNExplainer, IBGNN, CI-GNN) built directly on GNNExplainer/PGExplainer machinery, almost entirely applied to disease classification rather than concept-level, naturalistic-stimulus decisions. Concept-based interpretability (TCAV, concept bottlenecks) has only just begun to touch brain graphs at all — one 2025 preprint (Wang et al., concept-guided GNNs for neuropsychiatric diagnosis) is the only clear hit — and it has not been paired with structural-graph substrates, naturalistic dynamics, or forecasting. The clearest gap for this project is therefore the intersection itself: using edge-level, GNNExplainer/PGExplainer-style attribution to answer "which anatomical connections carried a TCAV-detected concept" for a model whose functional dynamics were generated (or forecast) over a structural graph substrate during naturalistic viewing — a genuinely under-explored triple junction rather than an already-solved problem.

## 1. Joint Structure-Function Modeling in Current Neuroimaging ML

The idea of using a fixed structural connectome as the propagation substrate for a GNN operating on time-varying functional signals is well established as a modeling pattern by now, and has moved from "graph classification on static functional-connectivity matrices" toward genuinely spatiotemporal formulations. Wein, Schüller, Tomé, Malloni, Greenlee, and Lang (*Network Neuroscience*, 2022; also arXiv:2112.04266) directly compare spatiotemporal GNN architectures for forecasting future fMRI activity where anatomical connectivity supplies the graph over which localized functional interactions propagate — this is close to the project's Direction A in spirit (structure as substrate, function as the dynamic signal) and its Direction B (it is explicitly a forecasting paper), though it is not built on naturalistic/movie data and does not carry concept-level interpretability.

A second, larger cluster of work treats structure-function relationship modeling as an end in itself rather than as an architectural convenience. Zhu, Cui, He, Sun, and Yang ("Joint Embedding of Structural and Functional Brain Networks with Graph Neural Networks for Mental Illness Diagnosis," IEEE EMBC 2021 / arXiv:2107.03220) fuse SC and FC into one learned embedding for diagnostic classification. More recent work goes further and asks how well structure predicts function, and where it fails: a 2025 bioRxiv preprint ("Quantifying Structure-Function Coupling in the Human Brain using Variational Graph Contrastive Learning") proposes a dual-branch variational graph convolutional network that aligns per-region latent representations across the structural and functional modalities via a contrastive objective — this is the clearest example the search surfaced of a self-supervised, cross-modal (structure-function) contrastive objective in exactly the flavor the project's owner asked about. A companion line of work (Zhang, Yeh, and colleagues, "Group-common and individual-specific effects of structure-function coupling in human brain networks with graph neural networks," *Imaging Neuroscience*, 2025, building on the older Structural Decoupling Index literature) uses GNNs to predict subject-level FC from SC and decomposes the prediction error into shared versus individual-specific components, and explicitly uses GNNExplainer to localize which regions/edges show strong versus weak coupling — this is a direct precedent connecting Directions A and C (structure-function modeling plus edge-level explanation of where structure "explains" function), though again on resting-state or task FC, not movie-watching.

For genuinely multimodal DTI+fMRI+sMRI fusion, Qu, Zhou, Calhoun, Zhang, and Wang ("Integrated Brain Connectivity Analysis with fMRI, DTI, and sMRI Powered by Interpretable Graph Neural Networks," *Medical Image Analysis*, 2024/2025; arXiv:2408.14254) build one framework that ingests functional connectivity from fMRI, structural connectivity from DTI, and anatomical features from sMRI over a shared parcellation (Glasser atlas), explicitly built for interpretability. This is close to the project's data assembly (paired structural connectome + functional signal) but is evaluated on standard scan protocols, not naturalistic movie-watching, and its interpretability machinery is region/edge saliency rather than concept-based.

On naturalistic paradigms specifically, the movie-watching fMRI literature has exploded in the last year, but almost entirely on the stimulus-encoding side rather than the structure-function side: work using HCP 7T movie data (the same protocol the project uses) builds voxel-wise encoding models from AlexNet/CLIP/VALOR-style stimulus features to predict functional responses (e.g., "Mapping the movie-watching brain with AI-derived semantics," *Imaging Neuroscience*, 2025-2026; "Comprehensive Neural Representations of Naturalistic Stimuli through Multimodal Deep Learning," eLife reviewed preprint / bioRxiv, 2025). None of the naturalistic-movie papers surfaced in this search incorporate a subject's DTI structural connectome as a graph substrate for the functional dynamics — the fusion in that literature is stimulus-to-brain, not structure-to-function. This is a meaningful observation: the movie-watching + structure-function-fusion combination specifically appears to be unoccupied territory, even though each half is well populated separately.

## 2. Generative Forecasting of Neural Time Series

This is the direction that has moved fastest since 2024. Three sub-threads are worth distinguishing: (i) sequence-model forecasting without explicit structural conditioning, (ii) brain foundation models trained with masked or autoregressive objectives that can be repurposed for forecasting, and (iii) explicitly generative (diffusion-based) forecasting, some of it now structurally conditioned.

Sequence-model forecasting: beyond the GNN-based forecasting paper already cited (Wein et al., 2022), a Swin-Transformer approach (arXiv:2506.11455, 2025) predicts ~7.2 seconds of future resting-state voxel-level brain activity from ~23 seconds of prior fMRI, showing that spatiotemporal brain organization is learnable at near-voxel resolution — but again with no structural connectome conditioning and on resting-state, not movie data.

Brain foundation models: this sub-field has produced a rapid sequence of transformer- and state-space-based pretrained models over 2024-2026 — BrainLM (ICLR 2024, masked-autoencoding on ~6,700 hours of UK Biobank + HCP fMRI), Brain-JEPA (NeurIPS 2024, a joint-embedding predictive architecture that predicts latent target embeddings rather than reconstructing raw signal, explicitly noted by its authors as a natural fit for future multimodal/structural extension), BrainGFM ("A Brain Graph Foundation Model: Pre-Training and Prompt-Tuning across Broad Atlases and Disorders," 2025, arXiv:2506.02044, which does use graph-based pretraining across atlases though not specifically forecasting), and NeuroMamba (Choi, Park, Kwon, Yoo, and Cha, NeurIPS 2025 BrainBody workshop), a Mamba/state-space backbone trained with an explicit autoregressive objective on 4D whole-brain fMRI patches — the closest published match to "sequence-model (S4/Mamba-family) forecasting of fMRI" the project's owner specifically asked about. None of these foundation models condition on a DTI structural connectome as an architectural input; they treat structure, if at all, as a downstream fine-tuning target rather than a conditioning signal.

Generative/diffusion forecasting: this is where the most direct precedent for Direction B sits. BrainWorld (Xia, Ye, Zhang, Pan, Wang, and Liu, arXiv:2606.17742, 2026) is a two-stage latent diffusion framework — a VAE compresses voxel-level 4D fMRI, then a conditional Diffusion Transformer forecasts future latent dynamics for up to 400 frames — and it is evaluated across 22 datasets including HCP Movie and other naturalistic-viewing corpora (CineBrain, StudyForrest). Critically, its "structural prior" is anatomical **sMRI** (T1-weighted morphometry) injected via cross-attention and adaptive normalization, treated as subject-level anatomical context — not a DTI-derived structural connectivity **graph**. This is an important and precise distinction for the project: BrainWorld demonstrates that structure-conditioned generative forecasting on naturalistic/movie fMRI already exists, but "structure" there means anatomical shape/morphometry, not a white-matter connectivity graph with edge weights between ROIs. A DTI-tractography connectome — with its explicit edge structure available for GNN propagation, masking, or explanation — has not, as far as this search could confirm, been used as the conditioning substrate for generative forecasting of naturalistic functional dynamics. Other 2025-2026 diffusion work in this space (e.g., "Scalable Diffusion Transformer for Conditional 4D fMRI Synthesis," arXiv:2511.22870; "Conditional Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models... for Subject-Specific Brain Network Synthesis," bioRxiv 2025) conditions on task/disease labels or aggregate connectivity statistics rather than a full structural graph, reinforcing that this specific conditioning choice remains open.

## 3. Graph Explainability Applied to Brain Connectomes

GNNExplainer (Ying, Bourgeois, You, Zitnik, and Leskovec, NeurIPS 2019) and PGExplainer (Luo, Cheng, Xu, Yu, Zhang, Wang, and Zhang, NeurIPS 2020) are both post-hoc, mask-learning methods: GNNExplainer optimizes a per-instance soft edge/feature mask to maximize mutual information between the masked and original prediction, while PGExplainer amortizes this into a trained parametric edge predictor shared across instances. Both were designed for generic graphs (citation networks, molecules) and have since been imported into neuroimaging fairly directly.

The clearest connectome-native lineage runs through Hejie Cui and collaborators: BrainNNExplainer (Cui, Dai, Zhu, Kan, Gu, Lukemire, Zhan, He, Guo, and Yang, arXiv:2107.05097, 2021) pairs a message-passing brain-network backbone with a jointly-learned, globally-shared edge mask that highlights disease-specific connections — architecturally a brain-specific descendant of PGExplainer's amortized-mask idea. Cui et al. followed with IBGNN ("Interpretable Graph Neural Networks for Connectome-Based Brain Disorder Analysis," MICCAI 2022, arXiv:2207.00813), which explicitly frames the problem as finding disorder-specific salient ROIs and connections shared across a patient cohort (rather than one explanation per instance, which the authors argue is less useful clinically since patients with the same disorder share network signatures). CI-GNN (Zheng, Yu, and Chen, *Neural Networks*, 2024, arXiv:2301.01642) reframes the explanation objective around Granger-causality-inspired subgraph identification, aiming to find the subgraph *causally* related to the diagnostic decision rather than merely mutual-information-maximizing, addressing a known critique of vanilla GNNExplainer/PGExplainer (that mask masks are correlational, not causal).

On the structure-function coupling side, the "Group-common and individual-specific effects of structure-function coupling" paper (Imaging Neuroscience, 2025) explicitly applies GNNExplainer to localize which regions/edges carry strong versus weak structure-function coupling relevant to fluid cognition, and separately reports structure-function decoupling patterns as disease markers (prefrontal/parietal decoupling in Parkinson's, temporal-pole/subcortical decoupling in Alzheimer's, prefrontal-parietal-temporal-cerebellar decoupling in schizophrenia) — direct precedent for "GNNExplainer-style edge attribution on a brain graph," though the "graph" being explained is typically the functional-connectivity graph or a structure-function coupling graph, not a DTI connectome edge set feeding a downstream generative or concept-detection model.

Concept-based interpretability intersecting brain graphs is the thinnest part of this literature by a wide margin. The one clear hit is Wang, Lei, Tan, Li, Rasero, Zhang, and Agarwal ("Interpretable Neuropsychiatric Diagnosis via Concept-Guided Graph Neural Networks," arXiv:2510.03351, 2025), which integrates TCAV/concept-bottleneck-style intermediate concepts with a GNN over ABCD-cohort functional connectomes, and does connect concepts back to specific functional edges. This is the closest existing precedent for the project's own CAV/TCAV-on-brain-representations approach, but it: (a) uses functional connectivity graphs, not a DTI structural connectome, as the object being explained; (b) is diagnostic/disorder-focused, not naturalistic-stimulus-focused; and (c) does not involve forecasting. No paper surfaced in this search performs edge-level attribution specifically on a *structural* (white-matter tractography) connectome to explain a *concept* detected via CAV/TCAV in a downstream functional/representation-learning model — i.e., nothing that plays the role of "PGExplainer for concepts on a structural brain graph."

## 4. Synthesis: Where Is the Open Gap?

Laid side by side, the picture is: structure-function fusion (A) is a mature, moderately crowded area with real self-supervised/contrastive precedent (the 2025 variational graph contrastive coupling paper is the closest analog to what the project might do methodologically); generative forecasting (B) is the fastest-moving area, with a state-of-the-art system (BrainWorld) that already forecasts naturalistic/movie fMRI dynamics conditioned on a structural signal — but that structural signal is anatomical morphometry (sMRI), not a DTI connectivity graph, which leaves connectome-graph-conditioned generative forecasting specifically open; and edge-level explainability (C) is a well-worn extension of GNNExplainer/PGExplainer to brain graphs (BrainNNExplainer, IBGNN, CI-GNN), almost uniformly aimed at disease-classification biomarkers rather than concept-level or stimulus-driven decisions, and concept-based interpretability has only touched brain graphs once (Wang et al. 2025), on functional rather than structural graphs.

The project's specific four-way combination — DTI structural connectome as substrate, HCP 7T naturalistic movie-watching functional dynamics as the signal, CAV/TCAV concept detection on the learned representation, and edge-level (GNNExplainer/PGExplainer-style) attribution back onto the *structural* connectome to explain *which anatomical connections* carried a detected concept, potentially in service of or alongside generative forecasting — does not appear anywhere in the literature surveyed, in that exact configuration. The individual components are each precedented; the specific joint occupancy is not. Three sub-claims support treating this as a genuine, currently-open gap rather than an artifact of search terms:

1. **Naturalistic movie-watching is essentially absent from the structure-function-fusion and edge-explainability literatures.** Those literatures overwhelmingly use resting-state or clinical task fMRI; naturalistic-viewing work is concentrated in a separate, stimulus-encoding sub-field (CLIP/VALOR-style feature-to-brain mapping) that does not incorporate structural connectomes at all. Fusing structure with movie-watching dynamics is therefore a comparatively under-tried pairing on its own.
2. **"Structural prior" in the one closest generative-forecasting precedent (BrainWorld) means sMRI morphometry, not a DTI tractography graph.** This is a substantive, not merely terminological, difference: an sMRI-conditioned diffusion model cannot support GNN-native operations (masking individual edges, propagating over anatomical paths, edge-level ablation) the way a DTI connectome graph can. Nothing surfaced does connectome-graph-conditioned generative forecasting of movie-watching fMRI.
3. **Concept-based (TCAV-family) interpretability has essentially never been paired with edge-level structural-graph attribution.** The one paper that does concept-guided GNN interpretability (Wang et al. 2025) operates on functional connectivity, is disease-diagnostic, and does not extend its edge-level story to the structural/anatomical graph or to a generative/forecasting setting. A "GNNExplainer/PGExplainer for concepts, on a structural connectome" analog appears to be an open methodological contribution rather than a solved problem — this is the most novel and most exportable of the three original directions the project asked about, because it sits at a junction (concept attribution + structural-graph edges) that neither the concept-interpretability literature nor the graph-explainability-on-connectomes literature has yet closed.

The honest caveat: this is a fast-moving area (several of the most relevant papers here are from mid-to-late 2025 and even 2026, meaning the field could close part of this gap within the same timeframe the project operates on), and a systematic gap does not guarantee the combination is scientifically productive — it is possible edge-level structural attribution of naturalistic-viewing concepts turns out to be uninformative if structure-function coupling for movie-watching concepts is weak (consistent with the coupling literature's finding that association cortex, which is heavily implicated in naturalistic semantic processing, shows the *weakest* structure-function coupling of any cortical zone). That finding itself, however, would be a legitimate and citable result, not a dead end — it would directly extend the structure-function decoupling literature (currently framed around disease markers) into a new domain (naturalistic cognition) using a new interpretability lens (concept attribution) that the field has not yet applied there.

(arXiv:2112.04266) — Closest direct precedent for Direction B with anatomical-connectivity-as-substrate GNN forecasting, though on non-naturalistic fMRI and without concept interpretability.

2. **Xia, J., Ye, W., Zhang, J., Pan, X., Wang, M., & Liu, Q. (2026).** "BrainWorld: A Structural-Prior-Conditioned Generative Model for Whole-Brain 4D fMRI Dynamics." arXiv:2606.17742. — State-of-the-art diffusion-transformer forecasting evaluated on HCP Movie and other naturalistic corpora; "structural prior" is sMRI morphometry, not a DTI connectivity graph — the key distinction motivating the identified gap.

3. **Choi, J., Park, D., Kwon, J., Yoo, S., & Cha, J. (2025).** "NeuroMamba: A State-Space Foundation Model for Functional MRI." 5. **Caro, J. O. et al. (2024).** "BrainLM: A foundation model for brain activity recordings." 8. **Qu, G., Zhou, Z., Calhoun, V. D., Zhang, A., & Wang, Y.-P. 9. **Ying, R., Bourgeois, D., You, J., Zitnik, M., & Leskovec, J. (2019).** "GNNExplainer: Generating Explanations for Graph Neural Networks." NeurIPS 2019. — Foundational per-instance mask-learning graph explainer; base method for Direction C.

10. **Luo, D., Cheng, W., Xu, D., Yu, W., Zhang, B., Wang, Z., & Zhang, X. (2020).** "Parameterized Explainer for Graph Neural Network" (PGExplainer). NeurIPS 2020. — Amortized, cohort-shared edge-mask explainer; architectural ancestor of BrainNNExplainer's shared-mask design.

12. **Cui, H., Dai, W., Zhu, Y., Li, X., He, L., & Yang, C. (2022).** "Interpretable Graph Neural Networks for Connectome-Based Brain Disorder Analysis." 13. **Zheng, K., Yu, S., & Chen, B. 14. **Wang, S., Lei, Z., Tan, Z., Li, J., Rasero, J., Zhang, A., & Agarwal, C.

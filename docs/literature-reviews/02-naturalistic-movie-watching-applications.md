# Naturalistic Movie-Watching fMRI: Applications, Comparable Datasets, and Stimulus Annotation Availability

## Executive Summary

Yes — movie-content-level information usable as class-condition cues or auxiliary labels almost certainly exists for the HCP 7T movie stimuli, and it comes from two distinct sources. First, the Human Connectome Project itself distributes a `7T_movie_resources` package on ConnectomeDB that includes `WordNetFeatures.hdf5`: a hand-built, second-by-second semantic annotation of the movie clips using 859 WordNet object/action categories, built following the methodology of Huth et al. (2012) and documented in an accompanying `HCP.7T.Movie.FeatureSpaces_kj2016.docx` file. This is an official, pre-existing, per-timepoint content annotation — the single most direct answer to the question, and it appears to be underused outside a handful of encoding-model papers (e.g., the movie_cpm codebase, and papers building semantic/topic features from it such as Li, Eickhoff & Weis, 2025). Second, at least one very recent paper (Li, *Imaging Neuroscience*, 2025/2026) generated its own independent, richer annotation set for the same HCP clips — 11 continuous 0–1 dimensions (people presence, faces, social interaction, dialogue, music, motion, brightness, valence, arousal, threat, narrative progress) via zero-shot prompting of a multimodal LLM (Gemini) over 20-second sliding windows — which is a directly reusable template even though its output files are not (yet, as far as could be confirmed) publicly released as a standalone dataset. Beyond these two, no evidence was found of HCP publishing shot-boundary, dialogue-transcript, or valence/arousal ratings for its 7T clips the way StudyForrest does for Forrest Gump; that gap could be filled by applying the StudyForrest-style pipeline (manual/automated shot segmentation, ASR transcription, crowd-sourced or model-based valence/arousal) directly to the (short, public-domain-heavy) HCP clip set, which is very feasible given the clips are only ~2–4.5 minutes each. Finally, purely data-driven "event" labels — needing no external annotation at all — can be derived by fitting a Hidden Markov Model (Baldassano et al., 2017) to the structural or functional time series themselves, giving neurally-defined scene-boundary segments as a fallback labeling scheme. In short: official coarse semantic labels exist and are directly downloadable; richer affective/narrative labels exist in at least one paper's pipeline (reproducible, not yet an off-the-shelf public file); and StudyForrest-style manual annotation or HMM-based self-supervised segmentation are the two credible paths to anything HCP doesn't already provide.

## 1. What Has Been Done With the HCP 7T Movie-Watching Dataset (Beyond the 2021 Precursor)

The HCP 7T movie-watching protocol scanned up to ~184 Young Adult subjects (176–178 with complete/usable data in most re-analyses) across four ~15-minute runs (MOVIE1–MOVIE4), alternating two runs built from Creative-Commons/independent short films ("CC" sets) and two runs built from Hollywood-film excerpts ("HO" sets, drawn from the set of films used by Cutting, DeLong & Nothelfer, 2010, in their studies of Hollywood editing structure). Since the Misra, Surampudi, Venkatesh, Limbachia, Jaja & Pessoa (2021, *PLoS Computational Biology*) GRU-based 15-way movie-clip decoding paper, this exact dataset has spawned several distinct lines of work:

- **Functional-connectivity-based behavioral prediction.** Finn & Bandettini (2021, *NeuroImage* 235:117963) used the same 176-subject HCP 7T release to show that connectivity computed during movie-watching predicts cognitive and emotional trait phenotypes (via connectome-based predictive modeling) more accurately than resting-state connectivity, and that clips with more social content give the best predictions. Their codebase (`esfinn/movie_cpm` on GitHub) is also the public location where the official HCP WordNet semantic-feature file is mirrored and documented — a useful cross-reference for reproducing the HCP semantic annotation pipeline.
- **Inter-subject correlation (ISC) and functional-gradient work.** A 2025 bioRxiv paper ("Patterns of intersubject correlations parallel organizational gradients during naturalistic viewing") uses two independent 7T movie fMRI datasets (including HCP) to relate voxelwise ISC to the principal cortical functional-connectivity gradient (unimodal-to-transmodal), finding that regions with higher ISC sit at particular positions along that gradient.
- **Heritability.** A 2025 eLife reviewed preprint ("Heritability of movie-evoked brain activity and connectivity") uses the twin/sibling structure of the 178-subject HCP 7T movie sample to estimate heritability of movie-evoked activity and connectivity patterns, extending trait-heritability work into the naturalistic-stimulus domain.
- **AI-derived semantic encoding models.** Two very recent, closely related efforts fit encoding models to HCP 7T movie responses using features extracted by large multimodal models rather than hand-built taxonomies: (1) Li (2025/2026, *Imaging Neuroscience*, MIT Press; also circulated as "Brain–AI Alignment in Naturalistic Movies") segments the HCP movie runs into 293 overlapping 20-second clips, has Gemini rate each clip on 11 psychological dimensions, and fits ROI-wise (360 HCP-MMP1.0 parcels) kernel-ridge encoding models — finding that Gemini-derived semantic features predict activity well in temporal/parietal/frontal association cortex (and that better-predicted individuals have higher fluid intelligence), but predict little variance in unimodal sensory cortex. (2) A related bioRxiv preprint, "Comprehensive Neural Representations of Naturalistic Stimuli through Multimodal Deep Learning" (2025), similarly uses deep multimodal feature extractors to build encoding models over HCP-like naturalistic movie data.
- **Individual-differences / topological dynamics.** "Mapping/Probing Individual Differences in the Topological Landscape of Naturalistic Brain Dynamics" (bioRxiv, 2024) uses HCP 7T movie data to characterize subject-specific topological signatures of brain dynamics during movie viewing, again on the same clip set.
- **Stimulus-selection effects.** Li, Eickhoff & Weis (2025, *Human Brain Mapping*) directly interrogate which of the 13 individual HCP 7T clips are best for phenotype prediction, and in doing so build their own derived per-clip feature stack — motion energy, RMS loudness, Whisper-based speech transcription, brightness, face-detection counts, word counts, and a reduced (297-feature) NMF-reduced version of the official 859-category WordNet semantic labels — combined via non-negative matrix factorization into three interpretable topics (object/concept categories, human actions, household items). This paper is the clearest evidence that (a) the official HCP semantic labels are usable off the shelf, and (b) other low-level audiovisual features (motion, loudness, brightness, speech) are readily extractable from the clips.
- **Structural + functional joint analyses on this specific dataset.** No paper was found that pairs HCP 7T movie-watching functional time series with diffusion-derived structural connectomes on the same subjects for encoding/decoding or joint modeling — i.e., the joint structural+functional design in this project's own dataset construction (structural connectomes + 7T movie ROI time series on ~174 overlapping subjects) does not appear to have a direct precedent in the literature reviewed here. Related but distinct work exists on predicting functional connectivity from diffusion tractography in HCP data generally (e.g., Sarwar et al., "Accurately Predicting Functional Connectivity from Diffusion Imaging," and joint dMRI/fMRI structural-network-refinement methods such as the 2018 *Scientific Reports* paper on function-specific structural connectivity mapping), but these use resting-state or task fMRI, not the 7T naturalistic movie runs, as the functional half of the pairing. This is worth flagging explicitly: it suggests the structural+functional-movie pairing this project has built is a genuinely underexplored combination rather than a replication of existing work.

## 2. Other Naturalistic Movie-Watching Datasets: What's Been Built On Them

Several other naturalistic-viewing fMRI resources exist and provide useful comparison points, both for modeling approaches and for how much richer their stimulus annotation is relative to HCP's.

- **StudyForrest** (Hanke et al., ongoing multi-release project centered on the German-dubbed "Forrest Gump," ~15–20 subjects, ~2-hour movie). This is the most heavily annotated naturalistic dataset in the field and is the best template for what could be built for HCP if official annotations are judged insufficient. Published annotation layers include: (a) shot/cut boundaries and depicted-location labels at three levels of abstraction, for all 870 shots (Häusler & Hanke, "An annotation of cuts, depicted locations, and temporal progression in the motion picture 'Forrest Gump,'" *F1000Research*, 2016); (b) full spoken-language annotation of the German audio-description and dubbed dialogue — >2,500 sentences, ~16,000 words, ~66,000 phonemes, with speaker ID, lemmatization, two levels of part-of-speech tagging, syntactic dependencies, and word-embedding-based semantics (Häusler & Hanke, "A studyforrest extension, an annotation of spoken language in the German dubbed movie 'Forrest Gump' and its audio-description," *F1000Research*, 2021); and (c) emotion annotation — 12 independent observers coding emotional episodes for onset/offset, arousal, valence, and discrete emotion category (Labs et al., "Portrayed emotions in the movie 'Forrest Gump,'" *F1000Research*, 2015, with later "perceived emotion" companion work). This stack has enabled encoding models of speech/semantics, ISC studies, emotion-decoding studies, and event-segmentation work all on one shared, richly labeled stimulus.
- **Sherlock dataset** (Chen, Leong, Honey, Yong, Norman & Hasson, "Shared memories reveal shared structure in neural activity across individuals," *Nature Neuroscience* 20:115–125, 2017; data on OpenNeuro ds001132). 17 subjects watched a 48-minute BBC "Sherlock" episode; a second group verbally recalled it during scanning. This dataset is the foundational testbed for shared-response modeling (SRM) and for Baldassano-style event-segmentation methods (see Section 4), and has also been used to build mappings between fMRI responses and natural-language annotations of scene content (Vodrahalli et al., *NeuroImage*, "Mapping between fMRI responses to movies and their natural language annotations").
- **Naturalistic Neuroimaging Database (NNDb)** (Aliko, Huang, Gheorghiu, Meliss & Skipper, "A naturalistic neuroimaging database for understanding the brain using ecological stimuli," *Scientific Data* 7:347, 2020). 86 subjects, each watching one of 10 full-length commercial movies (not clips) during fMRI, explicitly built to support data-driven functional-network labeling using ecological, feature-length stimuli. A related, newer resource — "A neuroimaging database combining movie-watching, eye-tracking, sensorimotor mapping, and cognitive tasks" (bioRxiv, 2025) — extends this style of multimodal naturalistic database further.
- **Cam-CAN**: primarily known for resting-state, task, and structural/diffusion data across the adult lifespan; no dedicated naturalistic movie-watching annotation resource comparable to StudyForrest or NNDb was confirmed to exist for it in this search, and it should not be assumed to have one.
- **Other relevant naturalistic resources surfaced in this search**: the "101 Dalmatians" multimodal naturalistic dataset (typical development and congenital sensory loss, *Scientific Data*, 2025) which pairs computational-model audiovisual descriptors and semantic embeddings with human-tagged event annotations; "Emo-FilM" (*bioRxiv*/PMC, 2024), 44 raters providing discrete-emotion and dimensional-emotion annotations for 14 short films watched by 30 fMRI subjects; the "Narratives" dataset (time-stamped word-level annotations for spoken-story fMRI, used widely for language-encoding models); and an open multimodal iEEG-fMRI short-film dataset with detailed speech/video annotation (*Scientific Data*, 2022). None of these datasets combine structural connectivity with naturalistic functional dynamics in a joint model either — the combination remains rare across the field, not just missing from HCP-specific work.
- **Modeling approaches applied across these datasets** generally fall into four families, all of which are options for a structural+functional joint model on HCP data: (i) voxel/ROI-wise **encoding models** regressing BOLD onto stimulus features (WordNet categories, embeddings, motion energy, audio envelopes, LLM-derived semantic scores); (ii) **ISC / inter-subject functional correlation** analyses that treat shared, stimulus-locked variance as the signal of interest; (iii) **shared response models (SRM)** and related factor models (e.g., "Fast shared response model for fMRI data," arXiv:1909.12537) that learn a shared low-dimensional space across subjects viewing the same stimulus, directly relevant to a project already doing cross-subject GRU-based decoding; and (iv) **event-segmentation models** (Section 4).

## 3. Stimulus/Content-Level Annotations for the HCP 7T Movie Clips — The Key Question

### (a) What HCP itself publishes

Confirmed clip identities (from HCP wiki documentation and papers using the dataset, e.g., Li, Eickhoff & Weis 2025 and Finn & Bandettini 2021): 13 clips split across a Hollywood set — excerpts referred to in the literature as "Inception," "Social Network" ("social net"), "Ocean's Eleven" ("ocean 11"), "Home Alone," "Erin Brockovich" ("brockovich"), and a "Star Wars" excerpt (widely cited as being from *The Empire Strikes Back*) — and a Creative-Commons/independent set referred to (in shorthand form in at least one paper) as "two men," "bridgeville" (i.e., *Welcome to Bridgeville*), "pockets," "flower," "hotel," "garden," and "dreary." **These CC-set shorthand labels could not be independently verified against a canonical, citable HCP source in this search** (they come from a secondary paper's clip-naming convention, not a primary HCP document I could fetch directly), so they should be treated as provisional pending direct inspection of `HCP_7T_Movie_Info.csv` and `HCP_7T_Movie_Clip_Timing.csv` (the two files the HCP wiki names as the canonical clip-origin and clip-timing references). Commonly circulated claims that the CC set includes titles like "The Present," "Big Buck Bunny," or "Class of 2015" could **not be confirmed** from any source found here — do not treat those as established without checking the primary CSVs.

Critically, beyond clip identity and timing, **HCP does publish one substantive content annotation layer**: a `7T_movie_resources` package (available via ConnectomeDB) containing `WordNetFeatures.hdf5` — a manually created, second-by-second (1 TR resolution) semantic-category annotation of the movie clips, built by having annotators tag salient object and action categories in each 1-second window, then expanding those tags through WordNet's is-a hierarchy, following the procedure of Huth, Nishimoto, Vu & Gallant ("A Continuous Semantic Space Describes the Representation of Thousands of Object and Action Categories across the Human Brain," *Neuron* 76:1210–1224, 2012). The resulting feature space has 859 binary/co-occurrence semantic dimensions per timepoint. This is documented in a file named `HCP.7T.Movie.FeatureSpaces_kj2016.docx`, mirrored in the public `esfinn/movie_cpm` GitHub repository. This is a genuinely usable, official, per-timepoint class-condition signal — directly analogous to the "movie-clip identity" label already used in the 2021 precursor paper, but far higher-resolution and semantic rather than merely categorical-by-clip.

### (b) Third-party semantic/scene annotations built on top of the HCP clips

Two published lines of work go beyond the official WordNet file:
- **Li, Eickhoff & Weis (2025, *Human Brain Mapping*)** built a broader feature stack for the same 13 clips: NMF-reduced (297-feature) WordNet semantics, motion energy, RMS audio loudness, Whisper automatic speech transcription, scene brightness, face-detection counts, and spoken-word counts. This demonstrates that low-level audiovisual features (motion, luminance, audio envelope, speech presence) are all readily extractable from the HCP clips even where no official file exists — the clips themselves (as short, largely public/CC video files) are the only prerequisite, and standard computer-vision/audio tooling (shot detection, ASR, luminance/motion estimation) can be run directly on them.
- **Li (2025/2026, *Imaging Neuroscience*)** generated an independent, LLM-based annotation using Gemini as a zero-shot semantic rater over 293 overlapping 20-second HCP movie clips, scoring 11 continuous (0–1) dimensions: people presence, faces-closeup, social interaction, dialogue, music, motion intensity, scene brightness, valence, arousal, threat, and narrative progress. This is not (as far as could be determined here) released as a standalone downloadable annotation file, but the pipeline (sliding-window segmentation + LLM prompting) is straightforward to reproduce against the same or a similar clip set, and is arguably the most directly relevant template for adding valence/arousal/social-content style labels to this project's HCP dataset.

No evidence was found of a published, dedicated shot-boundary/scene-cut timing file, a dialogue transcript release, or a crowd-sourced valence/arousal rating set specifically for the HCP 7T clips comparable to what StudyForrest has for Forrest Gump. That is a real gap relative to StudyForrest, not merely an artifact of search coverage — StudyForrest's annotations are explicitly published as standalone, citable resources with their own methods papers, and no comparable methods paper surfaced for HCP's clips beyond the WordNet file and the two secondary-analysis papers above.

### (c) StudyForrest as the template for building HCP-clip annotations from scratch

If official/third-party HCP annotations prove insufficient, StudyForrest's three-layer approach (shot/location segmentation, full speech transcription with linguistic annotation, and multi-rater emotion coding for valence/arousal/discrete category) is a validated, replicable template, and it is arguably easier to apply to the HCP clips than it was to Forrest Gump: the HCP clips are individually only ~2–4.5 minutes long (vs. StudyForrest's ~2-hour movie), several are Creative-Commons/independent shorts that may already have simpler visual/narrative structure, and modern tooling (automatic shot-boundary detection, Whisper-based ASR as already demonstrated by Li, Eickhoff & Weis, and LLM-based scene/valence rating as demonstrated by Li 2025/2026) substantially lowers the manual-annotation burden that the original StudyForrest team faced.

## 4. Event-Segmentation and Scene-Boundary Modeling as a Source of Derived Labels

Even without any external content annotation, neurally-defined "event" or "scene" labels can be derived directly from the fMRI (or, in principle, structural) data using event-segmentation modeling:

- **Baldassano, Chen, Zadbood, Pillow, Hasson & Norman ("Discovering Event Structure in Continuous Narrative Perception and Memory," *Neuron* 95:709–721, 2017)** introduced a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) approach that detects event boundaries as points where the multivariate activity pattern within a brain region shifts from one stable state to another, without requiring any hand-labeled events. This was validated using the Sherlock dataset and has since become the standard data-driven event-segmentation tool in naturalistic-fMRI analysis.
- Follow-on work has used this HMM framework to study **individual differences in neural event segmentation** (Cerebral Cortex, 2023), to detect **neural state transitions** more generally (a 2021 ScienceDirect method paper), to show that **repeated viewing shifts event timescales** and produces anticipatory neural boundaries preceding stimulus-derived ones (bioRxiv, 2025), and to link **event boundaries to memory reactivation** (Journal of Neuroscience, 2019, "Rapid Memory Reactivation at Movie Event Boundaries Promotes Episodic Encoding"). A parallel line extends the same HMM logic to eye-tracking data as a cheaper proxy for event boundaries (Boundaries in the Eyes, *Behavior Research Methods*, 2025).
- No paper applying the Baldassano-style HMM event-segmentation approach specifically to the HCP 7T movie dataset was found in this search, but there is no methodological obstacle to doing so — the HMM operates on any subject's (or subject-averaged) multivariate ROI time series and would give purely data-driven "event/scene" boundaries as an auxiliary label set, usable as class-condition cues even in the complete absence of external content annotation. This is a credible fallback or complement to the WordNet/LLM-based semantic labels described above, and could in principle be applied to this project's own 300-ROI HCP time series directly with existing open-source implementations (e.g., the `brainiak` Python package's `EventSegment` class, built directly on Baldassano et al. 2017).

## 5. Most Relevant Papers and Resources

1. **Misra, S., Surampudi, S.G., Venkatesh, M., Limbachia, C., Jaja, J., & Pessoa, L. (2021).** "Learning brain dynamics for decoding and predicting individual differences." *PLoS Computational Biology*, 17(9): e1008943. — This project's own precursor paper; GRU-based 15-way movie-clip decoding on HCP 7T ROI time series; the direct baseline this extension builds on.
2. **Huth, A.G., Nishimoto, S., Vu, A.T., & Gallant, J.L. (2012).** "A Continuous Semantic Space Describes the Representation of Thousands of Object and Action Categories across the Human Brain." *Neuron*, 76(6):1210–1224. — Methodological source for the WordNet semantic-tagging procedure that HCP's own `WordNetFeatures.hdf5` file follows.
3. **HCP `7T_movie_resources` package (ConnectomeDB), documented via `HCP.7T.Movie.FeatureSpaces_kj2016.docx`, mirrored at `github.com/esfinn/movie_cpm`.** — The official HCP-provided per-second, 859-category WordNet semantic annotation of the 7T movie clips; the single most directly usable class-condition resource identified.
4. **Li, X., Eickhoff, S.B., & Weis, S. (2025).** "Stimulus Selection Influences Prediction of Individual Phenotypes in Naturalistic Conditions." *Human Brain Mapping* (also bioRxiv 2023.12.07.570273). — Builds a full derived feature stack (WordNet-NMF, motion energy, loudness, Whisper transcripts, brightness, face/word counts) for the 13 HCP 7T clips; best current template for extracting low-level audiovisual features from these specific clips.
5. **Li, M. (2025/2026).** "Mapping the movie-watching brain with AI-derived semantics" ("Brain–AI Alignment in Naturalistic Movies"). *Imaging Neuroscience* (MIT Press). — Generates 11-dimensional LLM (Gemini)-derived semantic/affective/narrative ratings for 293 sliding-window HCP 7T movie clips; the clearest template for adding valence/arousal/social-content labels beyond WordNet categories.
6. **Finn, E.S., & Bandettini, P.A. (2021).** "Movie-watching outperforms rest for functional connectivity-based prediction of behavior." *NeuroImage*, 235:117963. — Foundational HCP-7T-movie connectome-based predictive modeling paper; also the origin of the public `movie_cpm` codebase that mirrors the HCP semantic-feature files.
7. **Häusler, C.O., & Hanke, M. (2016).** "An annotation of cuts, depicted locations, and temporal progression in the motion picture 'Forrest Gump.'" *F1000Research*. — StudyForrest shot-boundary/location annotation; template for building analogous shot-cut labels for HCP clips.
8. **Häusler, C.O., & Hanke, M. (2021).** "A studyforrest extension, an annotation of spoken language in the German dubbed movie 'Forrest Gump' and its audio-description." *F1000Research*. — StudyForrest full speech/linguistic annotation; template for a dialogue/speech-timing label layer.
9. **Labs, A., et al. (2015).** "Portrayed emotions in the movie 'Forrest Gump.'" *F1000Research*. — StudyForrest multi-rater valence/arousal/discrete-emotion annotation; template for affective labeling.
10. **Chen, J., Leong, Y.C., Honey, C.J., Yong, C.H., Norman, K.A., & Hasson, U. (2017).** "Shared memories reveal shared structure in neural activity across individuals." *Nature Neuroscience*, 20(1):115–125. — Introduces the Sherlock naturalistic dataset; foundational for shared-response and event-segmentation methods applicable to HCP-style movie data.
11. **Baldassano, C., Chen, J., Zadbood, A., Pillow, J.W., Hasson, U., & Norman, K.A. (2017).** "Discovering Event Structure in Continuous Narrative Perception and Memory." *Neuron*, 95(3):709–721. — The HMM event-segmentation method; a fully data-driven route to scene/event labels that requires no external content annotation at all.
12. **Aliko, S., Huang, J., Gheorghiu, F., Meliss, S., & Skipper, J.I. (2020).** "A naturalistic neuroimaging database for understanding the brain using ecological stimuli." *Scientific Data*, 7:347. — The Naturalistic Neuroimaging Database (NNDb); comparison point for a large (N=86), full-length-movie naturalistic fMRI resource with data-driven network labeling as its primary use case.
